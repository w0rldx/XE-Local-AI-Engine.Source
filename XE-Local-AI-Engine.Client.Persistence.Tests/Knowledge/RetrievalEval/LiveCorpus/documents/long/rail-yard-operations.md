# Freight yard operating instructions

These instructions apply to every shunting movement inside the freight yard. They supplement the national rulebook
and do not replace it.

## Responsibilities

The yard supervisor plans all movements and allocates tracks. The shunting team leader is responsible for the safety
of a movement once it has been authorised, and the locomotive driver may refuse any movement that he or she considers
unsafe. Nobody works alone between the tracks.

## Communication

All movements are authorised over the yard radio channel. Hand signals are only used within direct line of sight and
must be confirmed by radio before a movement across a level crossing. A lost radio contact during a propelling
movement means an immediate stop.

## Hump shunting

Wagons are sorted by pushing a train slowly over the hump and letting the cut wagons roll by gravity into the
classification tracks. The retarders on the hump slow each cut down to a safe coupling speed, and the hump operator
watches every cut on the monitor while the automation sets the route. Before a train is pushed over the hump, the
shunting team uncouples the cuts according to the cut list, checks that every hand brake is released, and confirms
that no wagon carries a restriction mark on the placard board. Wagons with a restriction mark are never humped; they
are moved separately with the locomotive attached. The hump operator may stop the push at any time with the red button
on the desk, and must do so whenever a cut is heading for a track that is not clear or when a person is seen inside
the classification area. The standard release speed for ordinary wagons leaving the last retarder is 7 km/h, which
gives a coupling impact that the draw gear absorbs without damage and without shifting the load. Radar sensors in
front of each retarder measure the speed of every cut, and the retarder closes harder if the cut rolls too fast. In
cold weather the grease in the axle boxes stiffens and cuts roll slower, so the operator watches the first cuts of a
train closely and corrects the retarder settings if cuts stop short in the classification tracks. A cut that stops
short is pushed into place by the locomotive, never by hand. Wagons loaded with long steel products are released with
extra care, because the load can slide forward if the coupling impact is hard, and the operator notes each such
cut in the hump log with the time of release. Grease on the rails near the retarders is cleaned every Monday by the
track maintenance crew, and the retarder shoes are inspected every week for wear and cracks. When the yard is
covered by snow, the switch heaters must be switched on no later than one hour before the first push of the shift,
and the snow crew clears the retarder area first. Tank wagons are handled with a stricter rule: their contents slosh
forward on impact, so tank wagons are released at no more than 4 km/h, and a tank wagon that arrives faster is
reported to the yard supervisor, who arranges an inspection before the train leaves the yard.

## Brake tests

Every departing train receives a full brake test after it has been assembled. The test is carried out by a qualified
wagon inspector, who walks the whole train and checks that every brake applies and releases. The result is recorded
on the brake certificate, which the driver carries.

## Dangerous goods

Wagons with dangerous goods placards are placed according to the separation table on the wall of the yard office. A
wagon with flammable gas is never placed directly behind the locomotive; at least one wagon without dangerous goods
must separate them. The yard supervisor keeps the list of dangerous goods wagons currently in the yard and hands it to
the fire brigade on request.

## Accidents and near misses

Every derailment, collision, or injury is reported to the operations control centre at once. Near misses are reported
on the yellow form within the same shift. Reports are anonymous if the reporter wishes, and nobody is disciplined for
reporting a near miss.

## Weather

In thunderstorms all work on the hump stops. In fog with visibility below fifty metres, propelling movements are only
allowed with a person riding on the leading wagon and in radio contact with the driver.
