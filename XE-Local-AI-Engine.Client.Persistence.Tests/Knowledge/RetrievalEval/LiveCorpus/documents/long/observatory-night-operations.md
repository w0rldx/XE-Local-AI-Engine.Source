# Observatory night operations manual

This manual describes a normal observing night at the two-metre telescope, from the afternoon checks to the morning
shutdown. It is written for visiting observers and for the night assistants who support them.

## Afternoon checks

The night assistant arrives three hours before sunset. The first task is to read the weather forecast from the
mountain weather station and the satellite images, and to record the expected cloud cover in the night log. The
cooling system of the camera is checked next: the detector must reach its operating temperature of minus one hundred
and ten degrees Celsius before any calibration frames are taken. Finally the assistant confirms that the dome shutter
opens and closes smoothly, because a stuck shutter at night is the most dangerous failure the building can have.

## Calibration

Bias frames and dome flats are taken before sunset. Twilight flats are taken in the evening twilight only when the
sky is clear, and the observer decides which filters need them. All calibration frames are stored in the night folder
with the date of the evening, not of the morning.

## Opening the dome and weather limits

The dome is opened at the end of civil twilight if the weather allows it. The night assistant alone decides whether
the dome opens or closes; the visiting observer may ask, but may not overrule the decision. The decision follows the
weather limits below, which are displayed on the large screen in the control room next to the live readings of the
weather mast. Temperature is the least critical reading: the telescope can observe well below freezing, and the only
precaution in cold nights is to switch on the heater of the secondary mirror so that it does not collect frost. Dust
is watched as well, because a strong wind from the desert carries fine sand that scratches the mirror coating, and a
dust warning from the particle counter means that the dome is closed even when every other reading is fine. Clouds
alone do not close the dome; thin clouds are observed through, and the observer decides whether the data are still
useful. Rain, snow or hail close the dome immediately without discussion, and the assistant does not wait for the
automatic sensor to trigger. Humidity is the reading that closes the dome most often on this mountain: when the
relative humidity at the mast rises above 85 percent, the dome is closed, because dew forms on the primary mirror
within minutes and would damage the coating. The dome may be reopened when the humidity has stayed below 80 percent
for at least thirty minutes. The assistant writes every closing and reopening into the night log with the time and the
reason, and a copy of the weather readings for the whole night is attached to the log in the morning. The live readings
are refreshed every ten seconds, and a sudden jump in any reading is treated as real until the sensor has been checked.
If the mast itself fails, the dome stays closed until the assistant has checked the backup sensor on the roof of the
workshop. The telescope may keep tracking inside a closed dome, so that the observer can resume at once when the
weather improves. Wind is the other limit that closes the dome regularly. The average wind speed matters less than the
gusts, which shake the telescope and blur every exposure long before they endanger the building. The dome is closed
when gusts exceed 60 kilometres per hour, and it stays closed until the gusts have stayed below that value for twenty
minutes.

## Observing

During the night the observer controls the instrument and the assistant controls the telescope and the dome. Target
lists are handed to the assistant at least one hour before the targets rise. Focus is checked at the start of the
night and after every change of more than two degrees in the outside temperature.

## Morning shutdown

At the start of nautical twilight in the morning the dome is closed and the telescope is parked at the zenith with the
mirror covers closed. The observer copies the raw data to the archive disk and checks that the copy is complete before
deleting anything from the instrument computer. The night log is signed by both the observer and the assistant.

## Safety

The dome is dark at night. Everybody carries a red torch, and nobody climbs to the telescope platform alone. In an
emergency the red button next to the control room door stops the telescope, the dome rotation and the shutter at once.
