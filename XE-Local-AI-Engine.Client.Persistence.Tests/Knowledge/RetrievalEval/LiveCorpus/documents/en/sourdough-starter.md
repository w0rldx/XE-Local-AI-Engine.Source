# Keeping a rye sourdough starter

A starter is a stable community of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria. It rewards a routine far more than it
rewards clever tricks.

## Feeding ratio

We feed at 1:5:5 by weight: one part ripe starter, five parts whole rye flour, five parts water at about 26 °C.
The high ratio dilutes the acid so the yeast gets a head start, which keeps the flavour mild.

## Timing

At a room temperature of 24 °C the starter peaks six to eight hours after feeding. At peak it has roughly doubled,
the surface is domed, and it smells of yoghurt rather than vinegar. That is the moment to build the levain for a
bake.

## Storage between bakes

Between bakes the jar lives in the refrigerator and gets a single feeding once a week. After a longer break, give it
two feedings at room temperature before baking, otherwise the dough rises sluggishly.

## Signs of trouble

A grey liquid layer on top is just alcohol from a hungry culture; pour it off and feed. Pink or orange streaks mean
the culture is contaminated and should be thrown away.
