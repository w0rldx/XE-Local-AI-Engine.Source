# Monitoring varroa mites

Varroa destructor is the parasite that decides whether a honey bee colony survives the winter. Monitoring tells you
when to treat, instead of treating by the calendar.

## Alcohol wash

Take a level half cup of bees, roughly 300 workers, from a brood frame, taking care not to include the queen. Shake
them in a jar with alcohol for one minute, then strain and count the mites that fall out.

## Thresholds

In late summer, more than three mites per hundred bees means the colony needs treatment now, because the winter
bees are being raised in August and September and must be healthy. In spring a count above one per hundred already
deserves attention.

## Sugar roll

Beekeepers who do not want to kill the sample can roll the bees in powdered sugar instead. The count is somewhat
less reliable, so repeat it a week later before deciding.

## Record keeping

Write every count on the hive card with the date. A rising trend across three counts is more informative than any
single number.
