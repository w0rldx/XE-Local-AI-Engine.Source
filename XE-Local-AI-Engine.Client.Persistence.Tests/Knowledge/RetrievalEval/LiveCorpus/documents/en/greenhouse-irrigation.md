# Drip irrigation for a hobby greenhouse

A small greenhouse loses most of its water to evaporation from bare soil, so a drip system that delivers water
straight to the root zone pays for itself within a single season. This note describes the layout we settled on
after two summers of trial and error.

## Layout

The main line is a 16 mm polyethylene pipe running along the back wall. Every bed gets its own branch with a
manual shut-off valve, so a bed of seedlings can be kept wetter than the established tomatoes. Each plant has one
pressure-compensating emitter rated at 2 litres per hour; cucumbers get two emitters because their leaf area is
much larger.

## Schedule

The controller opens the valve twice a day, at 06:00 and at 18:00, for twelve minutes each time. On overcast days
the evening cycle is skipped by hand. Watering at midday is avoided: the water heats up in the black pipe and
scorches the roots of young plants.

## Filtering and pressure

Rain water from the butt carries fine sediment, so a 120 mesh disc filter sits directly after the pump. The
pressure reducer is set to 1.5 bar; above that the cheap emitters start to spray instead of drip.

## Maintenance

Once a month the end caps are opened and the lines are flushed for a minute to wash out algae. In autumn the whole
system is drained and the filter disc is stored indoors so frost cannot crack it.
