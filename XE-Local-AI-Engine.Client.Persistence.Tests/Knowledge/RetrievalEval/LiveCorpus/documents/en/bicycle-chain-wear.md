# Bicycle chain wear

A chain does not literally stretch; the pins and rollers wear, so the pitch between links grows. A worn chain in
turn wears the cassette and chainrings, and replacing those costs far more than a new chain.

## Measuring

Use a chain checker gauge at three places around the chain and take the highest reading. For 11- and 12-speed
drivetrains replace the chain at 0.5 percent elongation. Older 8-speed drivetrains tolerate up to 0.75 percent
before the cassette suffers.

## Cleaning and lubrication

Wipe the chain after every wet ride. A full clean with a citrus degreaser is only needed when grit has built up.
Apply lubricant to the rollers, let it soak in for ten minutes, then wipe the outside dry: lubricant on the outer
plates only collects dirt.

## Replacement interval

On a road bike ridden in all weathers a chain typically lasts 3000 to 5000 kilometres. Riders who rotate two chains
every 1000 kilometres report that the cassette lasts noticeably longer.
